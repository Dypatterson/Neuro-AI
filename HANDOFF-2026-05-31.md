## The two findings that drive everything next

- **The consolidation write is a ROLE-selective associative MEMORY, not a learner**
  ([[neuro_ai_memory_not_learner]] memory). In-sample it recalls role→target bindings from
  sparse cues role-selectively (the graduation + G-D pass). Held-out, that recall **generalizes
  only on low-rank structure** (topic toy held-out role-Δ 0.42, structure-ablation bag→0); on
  **real text held-out recall ≈ chance** (0.05; no low-rank role→target rule to generalize at
  sparse cues). **This is correct** for a contextual-completion (NOT sequence-prediction) system
  — memorization-recall is the target. **G-D is judged in-sample (user-approved).** Report 055
  stands as the correct *memorization* graduation. Don't re-litigate the held-out null as a
  failure — it's the expected signature of a memory.
- **A clean abstract rule-toy provably can't reproduce the graduated mechanism's signature**
  ([[neuro_ai_design_panel_clean_toy_cant_reproduce]] memory; Forks 1/2/3). Future "minimal toy"
  designs should reproduce the *signature* on the masked-encoding substrate, not a clean rule.
