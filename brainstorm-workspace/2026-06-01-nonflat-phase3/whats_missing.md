# COMPLETENESS CRITIC — what the 7 generators missed

**Verdict in one line.** The 7 families are wide on *what carries structure* (vectors → factored codes → graphs → spiking groups → topologies) but startlingly **narrow on what drives the growth**: ~6 of 7 best-bets are some flavor of **structure/content factorization or a spectral basis of a transition operator** (TEM/SR/grid appears as a candidate in *all 7 families*; SFA in 3; k-WTA/sparse in 3). They diverged on representation and converged hard on mechanism. The biggest gaps are on the **driving-signal axis**, not the representation axis.

---

## (1) The whole FAMILY that is missing: NEUROMODULATORY / VALUE-GATED SELECTIONIST growth (a *second, slow, global-but-diffuse* signal that gates which local plasticity survives)

Every one of the 7 families grows structure from **one signal: the input/context statistics themselves** (co-occurrence, transitions, temporal slowness, spike timing). Not one uses a **second, orthogonal, slow neuromodulatory/value signal to *gate* which co-occurrence statistics get consolidated into structure.** This is arguably *the* defining feature of how the brain actually escapes "everything that co-fires wires together" — the brain does **not** Hebb-wire on raw co-occurrence; it wires on co-occurrence **× a third factor** (dopamine/ACh/NE eligibility-trace gating). The 7 generators collectively reinvented the *content* of the cortical map and skipped the **neuromodulatory system that decides what gets written into it.**

This is a genuine omission with three distinct, primary-grounded sub-mechanisms:

**(1a) Three-factor / neo-Hebbian eligibility-gated growth.** Plasticity = pre × post × **modulator**, where a slow eligibility trace holds the Hebbian co-activation pending until a delayed third factor (surprise/novelty/value) confirms it (Frémaux & Gerstner; the Frontiers 2018 neo-Hebbian review; the 2024–25 striatal-ACh-gating work). **Bio:** Gerstner, Lehmann, Liakoni, Corneil, Brea 2018, *Eligibility Traces and Plasticity on Behavioral Time Scales* (Front. Neural Circuits). **Why it escapes the bound:** the dominant collocational mode is *high-frequency and low-surprise* — exactly what a novelty/surprise-gated third factor **down-weights**. King-the-and co-occur constantly (low surprise → gated OUT of structural consolidation); king/throne is a rarer, higher-surprise pairing that survives the gate. The third factor is a **principled, local, non-co-occurrence weighting** that suppresses the dominant mode *at write time* rather than fighting it at read time (which is all H_anti could do). This is categorically different from H_anti/repulsion: H_anti is a uniform spread *after* the dominant mode is already written; eligibility-gating *never writes it*.

**(1b) Neuron–astrocyte associative memory (the missed substrate-level mechanism).** Kozachkov, Slotine, Krotov et al., *Neuron–astrocyte associative memory* (PNAS 2024) shows a **tripartite** system where a slow astrocytic process, integrating over seconds and over **spatial neighborhoods of synapses**, augments a dense associative memory — adding a *second slow timescale and a spatial-pooling transformation* of the stored patterns that neurons-alone cannot. The astrocyte network is a **slow, spatially-integrated, low-pass filter coupled to the Hopfield substrate** — and the project *already runs a Modern Hopfield network*, so this is the lowest-substrate-cost member of the missing family. **Why it escapes the bound:** the slow astrocytic integration is a literal physical realization of the fast(collocational)/slow(paradigmatic) timescale split that family D's Benna-Fusi candidate only put *inside the synapse* — here it's a *separate coupled network* with spatial pooling, which can carry a low-rank shared-context summary the per-synapse cascade cannot.

**(1c) Neural Darwinism / value-dependent selection (Edelman).** The substrate starts with a **degenerate, redundant repertoire** of candidate microcircuits (many groups that could represent the same token); experience does not *grow* a code, it **selectively amplifies and prunes** a pre-existing variation, gated by **value systems** (the neuromodulators of 1a). **Bio:** Edelman, *Neural Darwinism: The Theory of Neuronal Group Selection* (1987); the value-dependent-selection + reentry formulation. **Why it's architecturally different from everything generated:** family D's SOM/GNG/structural-plasticity *grows units toward the data*; selectionism *starts over-complete and carves away* — a fundamentally different dynamical regime (Olshausen-Field over-completeness meets natural selection), and the carving signal is *value/reentry*, not quantization error.

**Candidate sketch (the family's best single bet) — "Eligibility-gated consolidation":**
- **Mechanism:** keep the existing co-occurrence/SR machinery, but every replayed co-activation deposits a *decaying eligibility trace*, not a write. A **second slow channel** computes a local surprise/novelty signal per token (e.g. prediction-error of the existing settling read — the MHN already produces a settling residual; that residual *is* a local surprise signal, no new homunculus). Structure is consolidated as eligibility **× surprise**, integrated over the sleep pass.
- **Anti-homunculus:** clean *if* the third factor is a genuine local energy/surprise quantity (the MHN settling residual, or a Benna-Fusi slow variable), not an `if-rare-then-write` rule. **This is the sharpest tension to flag** — the third factor must be a measured local dynamic, exactly the line CLAUDE.md draws.
- **Batch-offline:** native — eligibility traces are *the* canonical mechanism for bridging a fast event to a delayed sleep-phase consolidation signal. This is the *most* sleep/wake-native idea in the entire brainstorm and nobody proposed it.
- **FHRR-fit:** the eligibility trace and the surprise scalar are weights on existing FHRR writes — near-zero substrate change for (1a); (1b) couples a slow field to the existing MHN; (1c) is a larger change.
- **Bound-escape, sharply:** the bound (Report 123 §4) is that *local iteration of one operator amplifies the dominant mode*. Eligibility-gating **changes the operator being iterated** from `cooc` to `cooc ⊙ surprise` — and surprise is **anti-correlated with frequency**, which is precisely the `corr(cooc, drift) < 0.15` gate the project keeps failing. This is the only proposal that attacks the failing gate's quantity *by construction*.
- **Cheap test (decisive, ~an afternoon, reuses the 123 harness):** re-weight each co-occurrence count by a surprise factor (e.g. PMI-surprisal or 1/freq, or the held-out next-token cross-entropy of that context) *before* the local `grow_G` iteration, then run the existing gauge-free gate. If surprise-weighting moves collapse-free specificity off the +0.021 ceiling *and* drops `corr(cooc,drift)` below 0.15 — where every un-gated growth rule failed — the family is greenlit. **Kill criterion:** if surprise-weighting leaves `corr > 0.15`, the third-factor premise is wrong and the family dies cheaply. *(Honest caveat: surprise-weighting is close to what SPPMI's PMI term already does — the cheap test must show the **eligibility × surprise** interaction beats plain SPPMI, or it collapses to 123.)*
- **REINVENTS-KILLED?** Flag honestly: PMI-style surprise weighting is *already inside SPPMI* (Report 123's operator). The non-trivial new claim is the **decoupling of write-eligibility from read-statistics across two timescales** (1a/1b) — that the *interaction* of a slow eligibility trace and a delayed surprise signal reaches structure that a single static re-weighting cannot. This must be the pre-committed discriminator or it relapses into 123.

A second missing family, smaller but real: **structure carved by GROUNDING / sensorimotor-affordance / value rather than by distributional statistics at all** (Object-Action Complexes; the affordance-abstraction literature; the embodied-semantics view that king/queen share structure because they *afford the same actions/roles in the world*, a signal orthogonal to text co-occurrence). This is out of reach for a text-only corpus, so I flag it as *correctly* unaddressed but note it explains *why* the text-only bound may be fundamental (see §4).

## (2) Ideas that are secretly the SAME idea, or secretly collapse back to co-occurrence (the bound's trap)

**The single most important finding: TEM/structure-content factorization + SR/grid-eigenbasis is ONE idea submitted ~9 times across all 7 families.** It is: H1, H2, H3, H4(partial); C4; A3; T2; G3; Wildcard-A; Wildcard-D(partial). The families *think* they diverged but the best-bet of half of them is the same hippocampal-formation paper (Whittington 2020 / Stachenfeld 2017). The convergence is itself a signal — but it means the brainstorm has **far less coverage than 28 candidates suggests; it's closer to ~6 distinct mechanisms.** The curator must not mistake repetition for robustness.

**Three named collapse risks — candidates that re-enter the bound's trap:**

- **The entire SR/successor-operator cluster (H1, C4, T2, G3) is at acute risk of being Report 124 re-run.** Report 124 *already tested a directional/successor operator* and got NULL, with the explicit finding that *the bound holds regardless of directional asymmetry.* H1/C4/G3 all say "but we'll take the **eigenbasis** of the SR, not the raw operator." **This is a real distinction only if the SR eigendecomposition is computed by a genuinely local replay-power-iteration — and here is the trap nobody flagged loudly enough:** power-iteration *converges to the DOMINANT eigenvector*, which is exactly the collocational mode the bound says is the problem. To reach the *subdominant* paradigmatic modes you must either deflate the top modes (a global operation = the SVD homunculus the project banned) or run the iteration in a regime that doesn't converge to the top mode. **None of the four SR candidates resolved this.** "Replay-driven power iteration reaches the subdominant modes via local dynamics" is asserted in H1 but is *mechanistically false as stated* — vanilla power iteration provably reaches the dominant mode. This is the deepest unexamined hole in the most-popular idea.

- **G2 (Bio-SFA) and H1 (SR) are the same eigenproblem.** Sprekeler's own result (cited by family H) is that **SFA ≡ SR** — slow features *are* the subdominant eigenvectors of the transition operator. So G2's "spectrally-inverted, reaches the bottom of the spectrum" and H1's "subdominant SR modes" are **one mechanism described from two ends.** And both inherit the same trap: Bio-SFA's local Hebbian/anti-Hebbian rule is a *generalized* eigenproblem solver whose convergence to the slow (subdominant) modes is exactly the thing in question. G2 is more honest about it ("the test must show the local rule approaches the closed form"), which makes G2 the better-specified twin — but the curator should treat G2 and H1 as **one bet to test once**, not two.

- **A1 (assembly k-WTA), C2 (cerebellar expansion k-WTA), and C1 (NSM rectification) are three faces of "add a sparsifying nonlinearity."** All three escape the bound via the *same* claimed mechanism: a rectify/cap nonlinearity clips the dense common mode. They are not independent confirmations; they are one hypothesis ("nonlinearity defeats the linear-iteration bound") at three sparsity levels. **Collapse-to-co-occurrence risk:** C2 explicitly warns that linear Hebbian accumulation *in the expanded space* re-enters the bound — i.e. the expansion alone buys nothing; the k-WTA is load-bearing. The same is silently true of A1 and C1. If the cheap test for any one of them keeps the nonlinearity but still reads `corr > 0.15`, all three are dead together.

**Two candidates that quietly ARE the killed flat code:**
- **A2 (structured-Hopfield W_rel)** and **A4 (map-stitching)** both admit in their own §7 that if the coupling/anchor signal is first-order co-occurrence they collapse to Report 121. They are flat-code-with-a-relational-term; the term has to be grown from something *other* than co-occurrence, which just relocates the entire problem into "how do you grow W_rel" — unanswered.

## (3) The single most UNDER-EXPLORED, highest-upside bet — and why it's under-explored

**The eligibility-gated / three-factor consolidation mechanism (§1a) — and specifically the move of changing the *operator being iterated* from `cooc` to `cooc ⊙ surprise`, where surprise is a *local settling-residual* the existing MHN already produces.**

**Why it's the highest-upside:** it is the *only* proposal in the entire brainstorm that attacks the **exact failing quantity** — `corr(cooc, drift) < 0.15` — by construction, rather than hoping a richer representation incidentally decorrelates from frequency. Every other candidate escapes the bound by changing *what carries* the structure and *hopes* the result decorrelates from co-occurrence; this one changes *what is written* so that the dominant collocational mode is **suppressed at the source by a signal that is anti-correlated with frequency.** The bound is fundamentally a statement that "frequency dominates"; a frequency-anti-correlated gating signal is the most direct possible answer.

**Why it's under-explored (and this is diagnostic):** all 7 generators were anchored to "grow a representation from input statistics," because that is the shape of *every prior report* (121/123/124 all iterate an operator on a code). The third-factor framing requires a **conceptual reframe from "what do we encode" to "what do we let through"** — a gating/selection frame rather than an encoding frame. The brainstorm's own §2 convergence on TEM shows the generators were pulled toward the most *famous* hippocampal result (the cognitive map) and away from the *less glamorous but more mechanistically load-bearing* one (the neuromodulatory gate that decides what enters the map). It is also under-explored because the project's own MEMORY explicitly **bans online error-driven writes** — and "three-factor learning" *sounds* like error-driven online learning, so it likely got reflexively filtered out. But that is a misread: eligibility-gated **consolidation** is offline/replay by construction (eligibility traces exist *precisely* to bridge a fast event to a delayed offline signal), so it is **fully compliant** — the ban is on the runtime write, not on the existence of a third factor. This reflexive filter is exactly the kind of premature self-censorship the brief warned against.

**Runner-up under-explored bet:** the **neuron–astrocyte associative memory (§1b, Kozachkov/Krotov PNAS 2024)** — because it is the one missing-family member that is a **drop-in coupling to the project's *existing* Modern Hopfield substrate**, adds the fast/slow timescale split as a *separate coupled field with spatial pooling* (not buried in a synapse), and comes from the same Krotov dense-associative-memory lineage the project already builds on. Lowest-surprise integration, genuinely novel structure-carrying mechanism, and almost nobody outside one PNAS paper has touched it.

## (4) Is the goal "grow paradigmatic structure" MIS-POSED? A reframe that dissolves it

**Yes — and this is the finding I'd most want surfaced. There are two distinct reframes, one local and one fundamental.**

**Reframe A (the load-bearing one): the project is trying to grow paradigmatic structure from a corpus in which it may not be *first-order recoverable at all by any local rule*, because paradigmatic structure is defined by SUBSTITUTABILITY, and substitutability is a SECOND-ORDER, COUNTERFACTUAL relation ("king could appear where queen does") that is not a statistic of the observed stream — it is a statistic of the *unobserved* stream.** Report 123's bound ("it's in the subdominant SVD modes") is usually read as "we need a better local rule to reach those modes." But the **CLS literature (McClelland, McNaughton, O'Reilly 1995; the 2023 "Organizing memories for generalization in CLS")** offers a sharper reading: the neocortex extracts shared structure **only through slow, heavily *interleaved* replay that mixes episodes across contexts** — generalization is a property of the *replay schedule and interleaving*, not of the per-item growth rule. **None of the 7 families touched the replay *curriculum* as the locus of structure.** They all assumed the structure is latent in the static operator and the job is to decode it; CLS says the structure is *manufactured by the interleaving dynamics of replay itself* — replaying king-contexts and queen-contexts **interleaved and pattern-separated** is what builds the shared abstraction, and a non-interleaved or non-separated replay (which is what the current consolidation does) provably *cannot*, regardless of the growth rule. **This dissolves the bound differently:** the fix isn't a richer operator, it's a **richer replay schedule** (interleaving + hippocampal pattern-separation feeding cortical pattern-completion) — and that is *cheaply testable* by manipulating only the replay ordering in the existing pipeline, changing nothing about the growth rule. This is the cheapest possible high-upside probe and **all 7 generators missed it because they were anchored on the representation, not the replay dynamics.**

**Reframe B (the deeper, harder one): "paradigmatic structure" may be the wrong capability target entirely, and the right bio-target is *role-binding in the completion task*, not similarity in the codebook.** CONTEXT.md §2 says the system is a **contextual-completion machine** ("what fills this gap"), and the *original Phase-5 gate* is **analogical retrieval** ("similar structural shape, different content") — **not** "king and queen have similar vectors." The brainstorm inherited the operationalization from Reports 121/123/124, which measure paradigmatic structure as **cosine similarity of static codebook vectors** (the king/queen lift). But that may be a category error: king and queen being *close in the codebook* is a *flat-code* notion of paradigmatic structure — the very thing the bound says is unreachable — whereas the *capability* the project actually needs is that **king and queen are interchangeable in a completion/binding**, i.e. they unbind to the same role under the same context. **A relational/binding code (Wildcard-C's role-profile, Kanerva's "Dollar of Mexico") does not require king and queen to be near each other at all** — it requires them to *complete the same gap*. This dissolves the bound by **abandoning the cosine-similarity metric** the bound is stated in: the +0.109/+0.021 numbers are *all* statements about static-vector similarity, and a completion-based target (does the substrate settle to "queen" when given the king-context with the king unbound?) is **measured by recall, not cosine**, and is not subject to the subdominant-modes bound at all because it never forms the centroid. **The mis-posing is metric-level:** the project may be measuring substitutability as *codebook geometry* when its own charter defines the capability as *completion behavior.* The cheap test: take the existing graduated heteroassociative memory (055–058, which already does role-selective completion), and ask directly whether king-context completions admit queen as a low-energy alternative — a *behavioral* substitutability probe that bypasses the codebook-cosine bound entirely.

**My strongest single recommendation to the curator:** before building any new substrate, run the two near-free probes that the 7 families' representation-anchoring caused them to skip — **(i) the replay-interleaving manipulation (Reframe A)** and **(ii) the completion-based substitutability probe on the existing 055–058 memory (Reframe B)**. Both test whether the bound is even the right wall to climb, and both reuse existing machinery. If either dissolves the problem, the entire substrate-redesign effort is mis-targeted. Among new substrates, the missing **eligibility-gated/three-factor consolidation family (§1)** is the highest-upside genuinely-new bet, and **neuron–astrocyte associative memory (§1b)** is its lowest-integration-cost member.
